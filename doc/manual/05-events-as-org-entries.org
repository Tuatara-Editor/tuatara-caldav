#+title: Events as Org entries
#+language: en

* Events as Org entries

How an event or task of a calendar is written as an Org heading.

Each event or task is one heading. Its UID is kept in the =UID= property.

- =SUMMARY= is the heading.
- =DTSTART=, =DTEND= and =DURATION= are an active timestamp on your computer's
  clock, such as =<2026-11-03 Tue 09:00-10:00>=, a range for several days.
- A weekly, monthly or yearly rule that Org's repeater can say is the repeater:
  =+1w=, =+2w=, =+1m=, =+1y=.
- A rule such as the second Tuesday, Mondays and Fridays, every other week on
  two days, three times only, or until a day is a date by rule, as the repeat
  menu of the date prompt writes it (~C-c C-s~, then ~C-r~): =<%%(and
  (diary-block 11 10 2026 1 12 2027) (diary-float t 2 2)) 18:00-20:00>=.
- =EXDATE=, or a time changed alone, is a note in the logbook of the time
  cancelled or moved. Cancelling one time (~C-c C-x C~) and scheduling one time
  elsewhere (~C-c C-s~) write the same notes.
- A rule Org cannot say, such as the last weekday of a month, is each time
  written out within the days kept (a year back, two ahead, at most 500 times),
  with the rule in the =RRULE= property. These times belong to the calendar and
  are never sent back.
- A task's =DUE=, =DTSTART= and =COMPLETED= are =TODO= with =DEADLINE=,
  =SCHEDULED=, =DONE= and =CLOSED=.
- =LOCATION= is the =LOCATION= property.
- =CATEGORIES= are tags. =Public Holiday= is =Public_Holiday=.
- =STATUS:CANCELLED= is the tag =cancelled=.
- =DESCRIPTION= is an example block named =description=.

Read back, an entry gives the same properties. Several timestamps in one entry
are an event each, the UID of the second and later ones followed by their day.
Everything else the calendar keeps of an event (attendees, alarms, its own
properties) is kept as it was. Times the entry still says as the calendar did
are sent as the calendar wrote them.

Words from a calendar stay words. A title stays on one line, a description stays
inside its block, and neither can become a heading, a link, a timestamp, a clock
or a note the agenda reads.

** Calendar files

A calendar file is read into its components (a calendar, its events, tasks and
time zones) and their properties, kept as written, so a file read and written
again loses nothing. Lines are written folded at 75 octets, never inside a
character.

- Values are read when asked for: text with its escapes, dates and times
  (floating, UTC, or in a named time zone), durations, offsets and rules of
  recurrence.
- A time zone a file names is found in the file's own definition first, then
  among the system's (=/usr/share/zoneinfo=), the name checked to be a zone's
  name and nothing more. One is written for a zone by its yearly rule.
- Recurrences cover every part of a rule of recurrence, extra dates, dates taken
  out, and occurrences changed one by one. They are expanded only within the
  days asked for, and are checked against the examples in RFC 5545 itself.
